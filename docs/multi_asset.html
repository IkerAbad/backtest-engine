Out-of-sample result The two-asset strategy returned 34.2857% over 2018-11-21 to 2025-11-28, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.3258 and a maximum drawdown of -46.6757%. The static 50/50 SPY/TLT reference returned 91.2796%. The strategy underperforms that reference.

What is measured

This uses real daily histories for two assets. On the first bar of each month the strategy computes each asset's return over the selected lookback and puts 100% of equity in the winner, 0% in the loser. The lookback is selected on each training block and applied to the following, non-overlapping test block. The first test callback emits both legs; the second callback at the same timestamp is a no-op.

The engine values the two assets independently, marks the portfolio at their latest closes and charges every fill separately. The reference is a static 50/50 SPY/TLT portfolio, bought once at the start of the out-of-sample period and never rebalanced, with no costs.

Walk-forward design

Training window: 504 timestamps. Test window: 252 timestamps. Step: 252, so test windows are contiguous and never overlap. Lookback grid: 63, 126, 252. The winner is chosen by training Sharpe, then total return, then grid order. 7 folds fit in the available history. Across the test folds the strategy selected SPY 69 times and TLT 22 times.

Capital allocation and per-asset costs

Target weights are fractions of total portfolio equity. The engine sizes each order as (w_i * E - position_i * price_i) / price_i at the decision close and fills it at that asset's next open. The strategy uses w_SPY + w_TLT = 1, so gross exposure is 100% and cash is the residual after fills. The engine does not enforce a budget; the strategy is responsible for keeping the sum of absolute weights at or below 1.

Every fill is priced and charged on its own asset. A switch from SPY to TLT produces a sell fill and a buy fill, so the fixed commission is paid twice. Commission is fixed + bps * |qty_i| * open_i / 10_000; spread and slippage use that asset's open and volume. The table below is the actual out-of-sample cost split.

The trap is cross-sectional: it does not use a single future price, it ranks two assets with their next-bar returns. The detector compares the per-symbol target weights and finds the first month where the ranking changes. In the masked replay every future request returns None, so the strategy stays flat. Strict mode rejects the future reads before a raw report can be produced.
